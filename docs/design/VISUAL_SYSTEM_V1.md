# BDNA visual system v1

## Design intent

The public interface should read as durable infrastructure: serious, navigable, layered and structurally legible. It must not resemble a campaign microsite, charity appeal or conventional DEI brand surface.

## Tokens

The current implementation uses a restrained system:

- dark infrastructural surfaces for conceptual/system layers;
- warm paper surfaces for explanatory and operating layers;
- gold as a sparse signal for access, leverage and source-critical emphasis;
- borders and grids as the primary organising device;
- large editorial type for conceptual hierarchy rather than decorative imagery.

## Diagram grammar

### Life & Opportunity Pivot Map
Two realms are separated by a visible boundary. The Asset Key crosses that boundary as a wedge. The diagram must remain diagnostic rather than implying a linear user journey.

### Four-component system
Foundation, Fund, Asset Keys and Fonio are distinct functions within one system. Visualisation must show relationship and circulation rather than a hierarchy of programme units.

### Impact Areas
Impact Areas orbit the Logic of Access. They are modular domains selected for leverage potential and may change over time.

### Asset Key anatomy
Any future Asset Key diagram should expose:
- boundary;
- friction;
- wedge/intervention;
- risk redistribution;
- locus-of-control shift;
- form/flow integrity;
- evaluation at the access boundary.

### Passing-forward
Future visualisation should show circulation without repayment arrows pointing back to BDNA. Value can move onward, laterally or not at all.

## Accessibility

Gold is not used as the only carrier of meaning. Focus states are explicit. Reduced-motion preferences are respected. Dense system maps must collapse to readable stacked structures at narrow viewports.

## BDNA Lab

Lovable / BDNA Lab may explore higher-fidelity treatments against these constraints. Lab output remains non-canonical until reviewed and ported into `Motherpher/BDNA`.
